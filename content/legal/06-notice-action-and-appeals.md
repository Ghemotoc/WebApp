# Notice and action and appeals policy

**Draft for lawyer review, 17 October 2026**

> This is a first draft for a Romanian lawyer (avocat) to review before launch. It is not final and it is not legal advice.

## In short

- Anyone can report, with or without an account. A person reads every report.
- We confirm we got it, decide fairly and tell you what we decided.
- If we act on your content, you get our reasons and can ask us to look again within six months.
- Terrorist content removal orders are acted on within one hour.
- People who keep posting illegal content, or keep sending groundless reports, can be limited.

## 1. Who can report and what

**In short** (proposed)

- Anyone can report, with or without an account.
- You can report anything on Ghemotoc: stories, comments, posts, pictures, profiles, spaces and messages.

**Full text**

Anyone can report content on Ghemotoc: readers, writers, parents, teachers, rights holders, organisations and authorities. You don’t need an account.

You can report any story, chapter, comment, profile, space, thread, post, picture, embedded song or video and private message.

Reasons include: illegal content, harassment, self-harm, sexual content involving a minor, terrorism, hate, spam, copyright, “Copied from a published work”, AI writing that isn’t labelled and anything else.

How to report:

- signed in: use the Report button next to the content;
- without an account: use the public report form;
- authorities sending a removal order: use the report form and choose “Removal order from an authority” (see our Contact and company details page).

There is one form behind every Report button, so every report reaches the same team.

## 2. What to put in a report

**In short** (proposed)

- The exact link, why you think it’s illegal, your name and email and a good-faith statement.
- You don’t need to give your name for reports about child sexual abuse.

**Full text**

To report illegal content, the form asks for:

- the exact link to the content (the Report button fills this in for you);
- why you think it’s illegal, explained as clearly as you can;
- your name and email address, so we can tell you what we decided;
- a statement that you believe, in good faith, that your report is accurate and complete.

You don’t need to give your name or email for a report about child sexual abuse.

Copyright and copied work. Tell us which work you think was copied. For “Copied from a published work”, even just a title helps; the passage you selected is attached automatically. If you’re the rights holder, say so.

If a report is too thin to check, we may ask you for more. A full report helps us act faster. A report with every item above counts, in law, as telling us about the content.

## 3. What happens next

**In short** (proposed)

- We confirm we got your report.
- A person reads it and decides. The most serious reports come first.
- We tell you what we decided.

**Full text**

We confirm we got it. Straight away on screen, and by email if you gave us your address.

A person decides. Someone on our moderation team reads every report. We don’t use automatic tools to decide about writing. Pictures are checked automatically when they’re uploaded, but a flagged picture is only held back until a moderator decides.

The most serious first. Reports about a child at risk, self-harm or threats to someone’s safety, and orders from authorities, go to the front of the queue. Removal orders for terrorist content are acted on within one hour.

How we decide. We decide carefully, fairly and without taking sides. We look at the content, the report, the law and our Community guidelines. We choose the smallest step that fixes the problem: for example cutting out one unlawful passage instead of removing a whole story. We never change a writer’s words.

What we may do. No action; remove or hide the content; cut out the exact part that breaks the rules; limit who can see it or raise its rating; add a label or a note; warn the account; suspend it; or close it.

We tell you. You hear what we decided, and how to challenge it if you disagree. The person who posted the content also hears from us, as explained next.

If someone is in danger. If a report makes us suspect a crime that threatens someone’s life or safety, we tell the police or other authorities straight away.

## 4. If we act on your content

**In short** (proposed)

- We tell you what we did and why, at the latest when it happens.
- Our message says which rule or law we used, whether any automatic tool was involved and how to challenge it.

**Full text**

If we remove, hide, limit or label your content, or warn, suspend or close your account, we send you a statement of reasons, in your language, at the latest when the decision takes effect. It says:

- what we did, and whether it applies everywhere or only in some countries, and for how long;
- the facts we based it on, including whether we acted on a report or on our own;
- whether any automatic tool was involved (for example the picture check);
- the law, or the rule in our terms or Community guidelines, that we relied on;
- how to challenge the decision.

We don’t send a statement if the law or an authority tells us not to, for example to protect an investigation, or if the content was spam sent in bulk.

You can see every statement about you in Settings, and we keep a copy with the case.

## 5. Asking us to look again

**In short** (proposed)

- You can appeal within six months. It’s free.
- Where possible, a different moderator decides, and we tell you the result.

**Full text**

If you disagree with a decision, ask us to look again. This is called an appeal. It’s free.

Who can appeal: the person whose content or account we acted on, and the person who reported it, if we decided not to act.

How: press “Appeal” on the statement of reasons, or use Contact us. Tell us why you think the decision is wrong.

When: within six months of the decision.

What happens: a person reviews it, where possible a different moderator from the one who decided first. We look at it promptly and fairly. If we got it wrong, we undo the decision straight away: for example we put your content back or lift the limit on your account. Either way, we tell you the result and our reasons.

## 6. Other ways to challenge a decision

**In short** (proposed)

- You can also go to court.
- You can complain to ANCOM, which supervises online platforms in Romania.

**Full text**

Appealing to us is optional. You can also:

- go to court at any time;
- complain to ANCOM, Romania’s coordinator for digital services, or to the coordinator in the EU country where you live, if you think we’re not following EU platform rules;
- [OPEN: whether we also accept certified out-of-court dispute settlement bodies under the Digital Services Act; we’re not required to while we are a micro or small company]

Copyright. If your work was blocked or removed over copyright, you can also use the courts and any mediation the law offers. You keep the right to use legal exceptions such as quotation, criticism, review and parody.

## 7. Misuse

**In short** (proposed)

- Accounts that keep posting illegal content can be suspended, after a warning.
- People who keep sending groundless reports can have their reporting paused.

**Full text**

Posting illegal content again and again. If an account often posts content that is clearly illegal, we warn it first and then suspend it for a reasonable time. For the most serious cases, such as sexual content involving a child, we may close the account straight away.

Sending groundless reports again and again. If someone often sends reports or appeals that are clearly groundless, we warn them first and may then pause their reporting for a reasonable time.

How we decide. A person looks at the whole picture: how many times it happened, how serious it was, how recent it was and what the person meant to do. We send a statement of reasons and you can appeal.

## 8. Orders from authorities

**In short** (proposed)

- Terrorist content removal orders are acted on within one hour.
- We tell authorities what we did with their order.

**Full text**

Removal orders for terrorist content. Competent authorities in the EU can order us to remove terrorist content. We act within one hour of receiving the order, keep the removed content for six months as the law requires and tell the person who posted it, unless the authority tells us not to.

Other orders. If a judge or another authority orders us to act against illegal content or to give information about a user, we check the order, act on it and tell the authority what we did and when. We tell the user too, unless the order says we mustn’t.

Where to send orders: see our Contact and company details page.

## Drafting notes for the lawyer

- Decision: this is its own document, not only part of the Terms. Reasons: the people who send notices (members of the public, rights holders, authorities) never accept our terms; DSA art. 16 to 20 content is long and procedural, and would double the Terms’ moderation section; the report form, statements of reasons and the Contact page can all link to one page. The Terms keep a plain summary (section “How moderation works”) and link here.
- The board has no tab for it yet. Suggested: a sixth tab, or a link from the Terms and from the report form. The “In short” lines in this document are proposals.
- Section 1 (Who can report and what): DSA art. 16(1): easy, user-friendly, electronic mechanism for any individual or entity. Report reasons and one form: S1-E10-01, S1-E10-02. A read-only account can still report (S1-E10-01).
- Section 2 (What to put in a report): DSA art. 16(2)(a) to (d) (notice elements), 16(3) (actual knowledge). Optional name and email for child sexual abuse offences: art. 16(2)(c), referring to Directive 2011/93/EU arts 3 to 7.
- Section 2 (What to put in a report): Copied-work report fields all optional and passage attached: S1-E10-08 (16 Oct 2026). Copyright notices and Law 8/1996 art. 128^2 if Ghemotoc is an online content-sharing service provider (L19).
- Section 2 (What to put in a report): The board has no tab for this policy; the “In short” lines here are proposals.
- Section 3 (What happens next): DSA art. 16(4) (confirmation of receipt), 16(5) (decision and redress information to the notifier), 16(6) (timely, diligent, non-arbitrary, objective; disclose use of automated means). Art. 14(4) proportionality; 1.8.5 passage removal.
- Section 3 (What happens next): Tiers: authority order 1 hour, child abuse material or threat to a minor or self-harm risk same day, other illegal content 48 hours, the rest 7 days (S1-E10-03, following the Q62 recommendation). Only the one-hour legal duty is promised to users; publishing the other targets is a choice for the founder.
- Section 3 (What happens next): DSA art. 18; Law 50/2024 art. 31 (S1-E10-10).
- Section 4 (If we act on your content): DSA art. 17(1) (measures covered), 17(2) (timing), 17(3)(a) to (f) (content), 17(5) (exception for orders under art. 9 and for deceptive high-volume commercial content). Applies to every hosting provider, micro and small included (1.8.5). Copy stored with the case: S1-E10-04.
- Section 4 (If we act on your content): “In Settings” assumes the statements are visible there; S1-E10-04 says they are sent through notifications (S1-E7-01). Please confirm the place.
- Section 5 (Asking us to look again): Appeals within six months, different moderator where possible (S1-E10-07). DSA art. 20(1) (six months, both the affected user and the notifier), 20(4) (reverse without undue delay), 20(5) (inform and mention art. 21), 20(6) (not solely automated). Art. 20 does not bind micro and small platforms (art. 19); applied as good practice.
- Section 5 (Asking us to look again): S1-E10-07 covers the affected user only. Letting the notifier appeal follows art. 20(1); please confirm.
- Section 6 (Other ways to challenge a decision): DSA art. 21 (out-of-court dispute settlement) is in Section 3 and does not bind micro and small platforms (art. 19); left OPEN. Art. 53: complaints to the Digital Services Coordinator; ANCOM is Romania’s (Law 50/2024).
- Section 6 (Other ways to challenge a decision): Copyright complaint and redress: Law 8/1996 art. 128^3 (as inserted by Law 69/2022), if Ghemotoc is an online content-sharing service provider (L19).
- Section 7 (Misuse): DSA art. 23(1) to (3) as good practice (art. 19 exemption); S1-E10-06 (Q83). Immediate closure for the most serious cases is a proposed default.
- Section 8 (Orders from authorities): Regulation (EU) 2021/784 arts 3(3), 6 and 11; DSA arts 9 and 10 (orders to act and to provide information, informing the recipient). Contact points: document 04.
