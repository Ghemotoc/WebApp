- This is the agreement between you and the company that runs Ghemotoc.

If someone tells us that something you posted breaks the law or their rights, we look at it as explained in “How moderation works” and in our Notice and action policy. You can always tell us your side.

## 4. How moderation works

**In short** (as on the board)

- Anyone can report a story, comment or message. A person reads every report.
- Every picture is checked when it’s uploaded. If the check flags it, a moderator looks at it before anyone else can see it.
- We may hide or limit a story, fix its settings or remove an unlawful part, but we never change your words.
- If we remove something or limit your account, we tell you why.
- You can ask us to look at our decision again.

**Full text**

Our own team moderates Ghemotoc, in Romanian and English. We don’t use outside moderation companies.

Reports. Anyone can report a story, chapter, comment, profile, space, thread, post, picture or message, with or without an account. A person on our team reads every report. We don’t scan your writing automatically and we don’t hold anything back before it is published: we act on reports. The most serious reports, such as anything that puts a child at risk, are dealt with first. How reports work, and what a good report includes, is in our Notice and action policy.

Pictures. Every picture is checked automatically when it is uploaded. Sightengine checks for nudity and gore. Arachnid Shield checks for known child sexual abuse images. If either check flags a picture, it stays hidden (readers see “Picture being checked”) until a moderator decides. The checks only hold a picture back. A person makes every decision.

What we may do. If something breaks the law, these terms or the Community guidelines, we may:

- hide a story or chapter, or limit who can see it;
- change its settings: where it can be read, whether comments are on, its rating (we may raise it, never lower it) and its other labels;
- fix its details: category, tags or language;
- remove an unlawful part, or someone’s personal information, by cutting out that exact passage and leaving a visible “[Removed by the moderation team]” marker;
- hide a cover that breaks the rules (your story then shows an automatic cover), or hide a title on public screens until you change it;
- add a note to a story we’ve confirmed was copied from a published work, or remove it;
- give a warning, suspend an account or close it.

What we never do. Our staff never edit your words, not even if you ask us to. You edit your own text. We never write a new title for you.

You always hear from us. Every change we make to your work is recorded, can be undone and comes with a message telling you what we did and why. If we remove or limit something, or limit your account, you get a statement of reasons: what we did, the facts, whether any automatic tool was involved, the rule or law we relied on and how to challenge it.

Asking us to look again. You can appeal within six months of our decision. Where possible, a different moderator looks at it, and we tell you the result. You can also go to court. See our Notice and action policy for every option.

Private messages. Messages aren’t end-to-end encrypted, so that reported messages can be checked. Our staff read a private message only when it is part of a report, or when the law requires it.

Spaces and class spaces. Space owners and the moderators they choose can look after their own space. Teachers moderate their class spaces. Our moderators can act in any space, and what they decide comes first.

Orders from authorities. If an authority sends us an order to remove terrorist content, we act on it within one hour. If we learn that someone’s life or safety is at risk, we may tell the police or other authorities.

## 5. How we rank and recommend

**In short** (as on the board)

- Popular lists are based on likes, reads and shares.
- “Popular in your area” uses the county you picked. We never track where you are.
- Items from our sponsors are always labelled “Sponsored”, and they never move a story up a list.

**Full text**

Discover is the main page of Ghemotoc. It mixes several shelves, for example Popular, New, Completed and New and rising, so new writers get seen as well as popular ones.

Popular. A story’s place on Popular lists comes from what readers do with it: reads, likes, comments, shares and how many readers finish its chapters. Recent activity counts more than older activity. Private downvotes are never used for ranking.

What we don’t use. No shelf uses your reading history, and nothing is chosen from a profile of you. Everyone sees the same lists for the same choices.

What you choose. The categories and tags you pick when you set up your account, or later in Settings, decide which shelves you see first. You can change them at any time.

Popular in your area. This uses the county or region you picked, if you picked one. We never track where you are.

Your age. You only see stories rated for your age. Signed-out visitors see only stories rated All ages and 13+.

Forums. Forum categories are pinned in a fixed order. Threads are sorted by latest activity.

Changes. We may change the mix of shelves and how much each signal counts, to see what works best. The “How ranking works” page always lists the signals we use, in plain words.

Sponsors. Items from our sponsors are always labelled “Sponsored”. A sponsor can’t move a story up a list, and a sponsored category or contest doesn’t change which stories are listed or in what order.

## 6. Ads and sponsors

- Ads match the chapter or page on screen. They are never personalised, for anyone.
- Ads are never inside the text of a story and never fill the whole screen.
- Class spaces never show ads, sponsored items or sponsor credits.

**Full text**

Ads and sponsors help keep Ghemotoc running. Here is how they work.

Two labels. Ads from ad networks are labelled “Ad” (“Reclamă” in Romanian). Items from our own sponsors, for example a bookshop or a public body, are labelled “Sponsored” (“Sponsorizat”) and name the sponsor.

Never personalised. Ads are chosen only from the page you’re on: its category, tags and language. We never use anything about you to choose an ad: not your age, your reading, your account or your device. This is the same for everyone. “Why this ad?” next to an ad explains this.

Where ads go. Ads can appear on public pages, such as Discover, Search, story pages, the end of a chapter, your Library, forums, public spaces and writer profiles. They are never inside the text of a story, never pop up and never fill the whole screen. There are no ads on sign-up and log-in, the age check, parent or guardian consent, your writing tools, messages, notifications or Settings. There are none on reporting and appeal pages or on the legal and help pages either.

Class spaces. Class spaces never show ads, sponsored items or sponsor credits.

What sponsors can’t do. Sponsors have no say over what we publish, how we rank it or what we remove. Before any sponsor item goes live, we check it is suitable for young readers: nothing about alcohol, gambling, tobacco, dating or anything similar.

Ad networks. We only work with ad networks that show non-personalised ads to everyone. Their code must not set cookies or read anything stored on your device. If a network can’t promise that, we show our own banners instead.

Your stories. Ads may appear on your story page and at the end of your chapters, but never inside your text. Writers don’t get a share of ad or sponsor income.
