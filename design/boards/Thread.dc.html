How do you start a chapter without a prologue?

Started by inkwolf on 14 October · 23 replies · 412 views

Pinned First chapters: read this before you post. Post up to 2,000 words at a time and say what kind of feedback you want. Locked: read only
i inkwolf 15 chapters Thread starter 14 Oct, 19:02 · Edited 14 Oct, 19:20 #1

Every version of my chapter one starts with two pages about the salt mine: when it closed, when it became a museum and why the choir rehearses there. My beta reader said she got bored before anything happened.

If I cut all that and open with the new singer walking into rehearsal, will people be confused? How do you start in the middle of things without losing the reader?

In news writing we start with the scene and explain the background in the third or fourth paragraph. Readers will wait for the explanation if something is already happening.

Our school paper has a one-page guide on this that helped me a lot: start with the scene (PDF)

A bit confused is fine, as long as they’re curious. What if the choir leader counts the singers at the start and gets one more than on her list? That tells us something is wrong right away, and the museum history can wait for chapter two.

I had the same problem with The Last Tram Home. What helped: I wrote all the background in a separate document, just for me. In the chapter I only kept what a character would actually notice or say out loud. Out of two pages I kept about five sentences, and nobody missed the rest.

c corbu 21 chapters Today, 09:47 #5

I check three things on my first page:

  1. Someone wants something.
  2. Something is in the way.
  3. There’s one detail that only fits this story.

If a paragraph of history doesn’t help with one of those, I move it to later. Worked for Night Bus to Ploiești.
