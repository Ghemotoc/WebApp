Why write here

You own your work. You decide where it goes.

  • You keep your rights

    Everything you write belongs to you. Ghemotoc only gets permission to show it, and you can publish it anywhere else.

  • You choose where it’s read

    Share a story on the web and in the app, or in the app only for more privacy. Use a pen name if you don’t want to show your real name.

  • No tracking ads

    We don’t track what you read to choose ads. The few banners we show match the page and are the same for everyone.

  • Community-led writing

    Readers comment on paragraphs and writers help each other in forums and spaces. After launch, we plan to add an optional supporter plan so readers can choose to fund the writers they love.

  • Yoti does the age check. We only get the result, never a photo.
  • Adults can never message anyone under 16. If you’re 16 or 17, messages from adults are off unless you turn them on.

The last bus from Bucharest leaves at 23:40. That night there were four of us on it: a girl with a cello case, a man in a suit, an old woman with a bag of quinces and me.

The driver had a thermos on the dashboard and the football on the radio. “Tell me your stops now,” he said. “After midnight I don’t stop for people who mumble.”4

The apps

Desktop and phone apps come later

Ghemotoc starts on the web, so you can read and write in your browser from day one. Free desktop and phone apps come after the web launch. They’ll let you read offline, use any font on your device and open the stories writers share in the apps only.

You do. You keep all the rights to your work. You give Ghemotoc permission to show it here, nothing more. You can publish it anywhere else too.

Yes. Reading and writing are free for everyone, and you never have to pay for either. Sponsors and a few small banners pay for Ghemotoc.

Everyone aged 13 or older is welcome. We’re starting with students, but you don’t need to be one. If you’re 13, 14 or 15, a parent or guardian gives consent before your account opens. From 16, you can sign up on your own.

Ads match the page you’re on, not you. Everyone sees the same ones, and we don’t track you to choose them. Ads never appear inside the text and never fill the screen. In a chapter, one banner shows after the last line.

Not yet. After launch, we plan to try an optional supporter plan. Reading and writing stay free. Any money paid to writers will come only from readers who choose to become supporters.

When you sign up, you give your year of birth, and the month if we need it to work out your age. Then you do a short age check with Yoti, an age check service. We only get the result (old enough or not), never a photo. You’ll still confirm your age if you sign up with Google, Apple or Microsoft. Google, Apple and Microsoft don’t tell us how old you are.

The minimum age is 13. Everyone gives their year of birth and does a Yoti age check. Under-16s need consent from a parent or guardian. Adults can never message anyone under 16. If you’re 16 or 17, messages from adults are off unless you turn them on. Stories are rated All ages, 13+, 16+ or 18+, and nobody sees a story rated above their age. A person reads every report, decides what happens and tells you why. You can report something even without an account.

A private space for one school class. A teacher with a Teacher badge creates it and gets an invite code. Only students with that code can join and see it. The class can share stories, run a writing contest and get certificates. Teachers get the badge when they set up their account, with a school email address or proof that they teach. Nothing in a class space shows on the public site, and there are never any ads or sponsors in it.
