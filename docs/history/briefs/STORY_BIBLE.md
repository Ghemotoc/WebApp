# Demo story bible (16 Oct 2026, evening)

Robert read the demo stories on the canvas and said they "don't make any sense". Every piece of fiction, blurb, chapter text, comment, forum post and message on the screens must now come from this bible, so the same story says the same thing everywhere. These are specs, not instructions beyond your task.

## Writing rules for anything you add or rewrite
- Plain, concrete, specific. Every sentence must make literal sense to a 16-year-old. Name the thing (a place, an object, a number, a time) instead of describing a mood.
- No poetic filler, no riddles, no sentences that sound clever but say nothing ("the silence remembered her"), no inanimate things doing human verbs ("the mine kept its secrets"), no "not X, it's Y", no pull-quote lines, no adverbs where a better verb exists, no em dashes.
- Warm and a little funny where it fits. Teen characters sound like teenagers, not like adults writing about teenagers.
- British English, no Oxford commas, typographic apostrophes (’). Numbers: zero to nine as words, 10 and up as numerals (dialogue can do what people say).
- Comments, forum posts and messages: short, ordinary, specific, like real people on a writing site ("the bit with the register gave me chills, is Ilie a ghost or not??"). Some lowercase and typos are fine in user posts; no corporate tone.
- Public-domain classics (Classics screen): show real titles and authors only. Never invent or paraphrase a quotation from a real author.

## The stories (canonical)

### The Salt Mine Choir, by inkwolf (Fiction, ongoing, 15 chapters, web and app)
**Blurb:** Every Thursday, 14 teenagers take the lift 200 metres down into an old salt mine to rehearse. Their choir director counts them on the way down. One night she counts 15, and nobody can say where the new boy came from, least of all the register.
**One-line premise (cards):** A choir rehearses in an old salt mine, and one night there is a singer whose name is on no list.
**Characters:** Mara (16, narrator in chapter 1, alto), Mrs Dobre (choir director), Teo (Mara’s best friend, tenor), Ilie (the new boy in a grey jumper).
**Chapter 1, “Fifteen” (opening, use for Reader chapter 1, Teaser, link previews):**
> The lift down to the salt mine takes four minutes, and Mrs Dobre spends all four of them counting us. Fourteen singers, fourteen coats, one tuning fork that she checks twice. At the bottom it is 12 degrees all year, so we rehearse in our scarves and the tourists think we are part of the exhibit.
>
> We sing in the big hall, where the salt walls go up like a cathedral somebody forgot to finish. The echo lasts six seconds. Mrs Dobre says that is the only reason we sound good, and that the day we sing above ground everyone will find out.
>
> On the first Thursday in October there were fifteen of us.
>
> I noticed because I stand at the end of the altos, and the end of the altos is me. That night it was a boy in a grey jumper, holding the music the right way up and singing my part a little better than I do.

**Chapter 14, “The register” (use for Share, quote cards, report dialog passages, paragraph comments):**
> Mara ran her finger down the page for each Thursday. Corina, Teo, Bianca, the two Stan brothers, herself. Fourteen names every week, all in Mrs Dobre’s square handwriting, all ticked. She checked September again, then October, then the week of the power cut when only nine of them came.
>
> There was no Ilie. There was no fifteenth name at all.
>
> “Maybe she forgot to write him down,” Teo said. He was whispering, and Teo never whispers.
>
> Above them the lift groaned on its cable. Somebody was coming down, and the register said nobody else was expected.

**Good quote-card passage:** “There was no Ilie. There was no fifteenth name at all.”

### The Girl Who Fixed Radios, by lia.writes (Lia Popescu, 16) (Fiction, ongoing, 6 chapters)
**Blurb:** Ana spends her summer in her grandfather’s repair shop in Ploiești. One old radio keeps picking up a station that stopped broadcasting in 1989, and tonight it said her name.
**Premise:** A girl repairs an old radio that picks up a station that went off air before she was born.

### The Weather Station, by lia.writes (new story Lia is creating on the New story and Editor screens; draft “Chapter one, untitled”, 1,240 words)
**Blurb:** My grandmother has written down the weather every morning since 1971. The day she went into hospital, I found she had already filled in the rest of the week.
**Editor draft opening:**
> My grandmother has written down the weather every morning since 1971. Temperature, wind, cloud, and one word for how she slept. The notebooks fill a whole shelf in the hall, and nobody is allowed to dust them.
>
> The morning she went into hospital, I opened that day’s page and found she had already filled it in. Tomorrow’s too. And the day after that, in pencil, with a little drawing of a storm.

### Small Rooms, by poppyverse (Poetry, app only)
**Blurb:** Short poems about sharing a flat with five people and one bathroom.
**Sample lines (only if a screen needs a poem):**
> My sister’s room is the size of the lift.

> and her winter coat on the door,
